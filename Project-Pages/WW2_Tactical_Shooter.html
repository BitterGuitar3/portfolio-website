About

This prototype is an early foundational step towards a large-scale tactical shooter inspired by Hell Let Loose, War Thunder, and Battlefield. The goal is to blend realistic infantry combat with realistic vehicle mechanics and immersive environmental destruction. However, this initial version is scoped down to a local 1v1 PvE environment, allowing me to focus on core gameplay systems without the added complexity of networking.

This was my first project using Unreal Engine and was built entirely using Blueprints. While I used a few tutorials to get started, most of my learning came through hands-on experimentation and problem-solving - including getting insight from other Unreal developers and online sources. The result is a rough but playable prototype which mirrors many core FPS mechanics, all designed from scratch.

While this is a personal prototype for now, it also serves as early groundwork for a larger project I plan to develop under my own indie studio in the future.

Features and Mechanics

  • First-Person Player System
    The Player can:
    • Equip, reload, and switch between two weapons (primary and secondary)
    • Aim down sights (with camera altering timeline and IK Systems)
    • Interact with pickups and ammo boxes
    • Sprint, crouch, and jump
    • Respawn shortly after dying
  • Enemy AI (1v1 Comat Logic)
    The NPC mirrors many player abilities and is driven by custom logic:
    • Shoots at the player with reduced accuracy over distance
    • Reloads and switches weapons based on ammo status
    • Picks up weapons and interacts with ammo boxes
    • Seeks cover when shot at or after spotting the player
    • Makes tactical decisions when low on ammo, including:
      • Reloading if possible
      • Retreating to safety
      • Charging at the player (in absence of better options)
  • Inventory System
    Handles two weapon slots (primary and secondary) for both player and NPC. Weapons can be dropped, swapped, and picked up dynamically.
  • HUD System
    A simple UI displays current ammo (AmmoInClip / AmmoInReserve) for the equipped weapon. The HUD updates in real time when firing or reloading and hides when no weapon is equipped.
  • Pause Menu
    A basic pause screen offers options to resume or quit. The game and AI continue running in the background, simulating how a pause would behave in a multiplayer environment.
  • Weapon System
    Guns exist in two forms - world objects and equipped meshes. Though currently using static meshes with no animations, the underlying system supports:
    • Interaction
    • Equip/unequip logic
    • Dynamic ammo tracking
    • IK-based hand positioning for realism

What I Learned

This prototype became more than just an exercise in feature building - it taught me how to scope, iterate, and plan for the future. I learnd:

  • How to take a loosely planned idea and shape it into something playable
  • The risks of starting without a scalable structure, and how to work around that
  • To recognize when to prioritize functionality over completeness (e.g, skipping melee and healing for now)
  • How to use AI systems in Unreal (like the behavior trees and EQS) to simulate tactical behavior
  • The importance of modular code and reducing reliance on direct casting or binding
  • How to approach IK and animation logic through both guided and self-driven exploration

Reflection

In retrospect, the project taught me just as much about design discipline as it did about systems and mechanics. I wanted to add everything - melee, healing, recoil systems - but I learned to cut scope when needed and focus on what mattered most at each stage.

The current prototype is rough in structure, I believe. There's a good bit of direct casting to obtain certain values and function that could possibly be reduced.There are also a few areas where logic might be overly event-driven. But now that the first pass is complete, I can use it as a learning artifact - either to gradually refactor and modularize or rebuild entirely with a stronger artchitectual plan.

Planned Improvements & Next Steps

Technical & Structural

  • Replace excessive casting with interfaes or event dispatchers
  • Create a shared base animation blueprint for Player and NPC for at least similar event data and movement animations
  • Optimize IK transform updates and animation variables synching
  • Learn and use modular data systems (JSON or data tables) for weapon configurations
  • Rewrite interaction logic to support hold vs press behavior
  • Clean up HUD by removing binding heavy elements

Gameplay Features

  • Add audio cues for footsteps as well as sounds for items hitting the ground
  • Rework hip-fire accuracy using dynamic spread system instead of relying on animation sway
  • Refactor recoil system to support recoil patterns and camera adjustment
  • Improve weapon drop behavior - have them detatch from their sockets instead
  • Improve NPC aiming - dynamically predict and fire at moving targets
  • Introduce class-based loadouts with support, assault, and skirmisher roles
  • Expand melee system (knife attack prototype)
  • Have camera stay with head upon death and have a respawn selection screen

AI Behavior Upgrades

  • Replace EQS-base cover with dedicated cover points
  • Develop basic flanking logic

Planning & Documentation

  • Write formal development plan for the next phase
  • Document communication and modular responsibilities between systems
  • Decide between a total rebuild or progressive refacotoring approach
