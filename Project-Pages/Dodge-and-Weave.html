About

This is prototype number 4 of 5 going down the Junior Programmer Pathway on Unity Learn. The focus of this prototype is to develop the basics of designing and coding gameplay mechanics. Dodge-and-Wevea is a arcade style sumo battle game where the player faces waves of enemies that increases in difficulty with each new wave. On every new wave, a powerup spawns that the player can use to help defeate the enemies, or they can chose to ignore it and save it for the next wave. To play, the player can use the A and D keys to rotate the camera around the center island. They use the W and S keys to push the ball forward and backwards. That is the only movement the player has to make it a more challenging task of avoiding the enemy.

Features and Mechanics

Dodge-and-Weave has enemies spawn at a random position on the island that chase the player down to try and push them off the map and lose. The enemy is always keeping track of where the player is an will be pushed in that direction of the player. The number of enemies that spawn equates to the wave number the player has reached. So if they are on wave 10, 10 enemies spawn. In each wave, for all the enemies that spawn, there is a chance it will be a normal enemy that the player can compete with in pushing off the map or it will be a larger and heavier enemy which the player will have difficulty in pushing.

There are 2 powerups that the player can get, pushback and rockets. When the player has picked up a powerup, and yellow ring aroound the player will appear. The player has 5 seconds to use the powerup they pick up any way they would like. The pushback powerup is indicated by a golden star that is on the island, and it allows the player to push back any enemy with a strong the collides into them. This is very effective with the easier and lighter enemies, but it is not as effective with the larger enemies. The rockets is indicated by a golden flame that spawns on the island. To use the rockets, the user only needs to press the F key for "fire". This will send a number of rockets equal to the number of enemies that are still alive towards each enemy. When the rockets collide with an enemy, it send them flying off the map. Once the user presses F and the rockets have fired, they no longer have the powerup, but they must also use it before 5 seconds has passed or they will lose out on the powerup. The rockets also have a time limit for them to hit their target before they dissapear as well.

What I Learned

The challenge with this prototype game was creating objects that can locate and follow other objects, like the enemies and the rocekts. Conceptually I had an idea of how it would be done, but found it a bit challengeing to put into practice. I learned that whatever the object is that is following another object needs to constantly know the position of what it is following and subtract it from is own current position. To make sure an object is looking at what it is following, in the case of the rockets, there is also a function for their transform property that is called LookAt that rotates the object in the direction of its target object.

Most of everything else that I did with coding was things I was already aware of and had used in previous projects. This project just helped me piece them together in a different way tho create some unique, but basic, gameplay mechanics that I may have never thought of how to do. Doing these prototypes have opened my eyes to how much you can do with so little. It is amazing what I could potentially do when I gain more experience and knowledge.
