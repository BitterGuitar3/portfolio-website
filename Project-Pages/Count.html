About

Count is a prototype created as part of Unity's Junior Programmer Pathway. The project was based on a specific challenge: create a system where balls fall into a container and are counted. After some brainstorming, I decided to give it a unique spin by creating a cannon-based prototype, inspired by games I used to play. In this prototype, the player controls a cannon that shoots apples into baskets, with the goal of counting how many apples successfully land in the baskets.

Features and Mechanics

  • Cannon and Power Mechanic
    • The player controls the cannon's shooting power by holding down the space bar. The longer the player holds the space bar, the more power the cannon uses to shoot apples.
    • The goal is to shoot apples into baskets to add to the count.
  • Power Bar:
    • A bar at the top of the screen shows the player the amount of power they are putting into their shot compared to the maximum available power.
    • This helps players understand how much force they’re using, so they don’t have to guess each time.
  • Baskets and Apples:
    • The player must aim and fire apples into baskets, but the challenge lies in hitting the target accurately and overcoming the force mechanics in play.

What I Learned

  • Clamping Object Rotation:
    • One of the most useful techniques I learned was how to clamp objects. Rather than resetting the rotation of objects every time they go out of bounds, I used clamping to lock the object within a specified range, simplifying the code significantly.
  • Working with the UI and Sliders:
    • I also gained experience with Unity's Canvas system, especially with sliders. Specifically, I learned how to update the power bar dynamically to reflect the power of the next shot.
  • Communication with Canvas Elements:
    • This project reinforced my understanding of how to update canvas objects, like sliders and text, from scripts. I also got more comfortable manipulating the UI to show real-time game mechanics, like shooting power.

Potential Fixes and Updates

  • Basket Models and Colliders:
    • A major issue I encountered was with the basket models. Due to limitations in the free assets available on the Unity Asset Store, I had to create custom colliders for the baskets. Unfortunately, the collision detection was not as smooth as I wanted, leading to some awkward bounces and difficulties in accurately shooting apples into the baskets.
  • Apple Physics:
    • Another area that needs adjustment is the physics of the apples. The apples don't fall as quickly as expected, and the force interactions with the baskets and targets are a bit too strong. Apples sometimes shoot upward with too much force after hitting the backboard, which feels unnatural. If I were to continue this prototype, I would address these issues to make the gameplay feel more realistic

Reflection

While Count was a relatively simple prototype, it helped me reinforce some key concepts I could apply to future projects. Despite some of the issues with physics and asset limitations, the project was a valuable learning experience, particularly in how to control object behaviors like rotation and power input.
