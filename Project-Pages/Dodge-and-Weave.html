About

Dodge-and-Weave is a wave-based arcade-style prototype inspired by sumo battle mechanics, where players must push enemies off a small island arena. This was the fourth of five prototypes built during the Junior Programmer Pathway on Unity Learn. The focus of the project was on designing and coding gameplay mechanics, particularly enemy AI and power-up systems.

Players use:

  • A/D to rotate around the island
  • W/S to push the player forward/backward

This limited movement design forces players to think strategically while evading enemies.

Features and Mechanics

  • Wave-Based Enemy Spawning:
    • Each wave spawns a number of enemies equal to the current wave count (e.g., Wave 6 = 6 enemies).
    • Enemies include both light (easy to push) and heavy (resistant to knockback) types.
  • Enemy Behavior:
    • Enemies spawn at random locations on the island.
    • Each enemy continuously tracks and moves toward the player, trying to knock them off the platform.
  • Powerups:
    Players can collect one of two temporary powerups, each active for 5 seconds once picked up:
    • Pushback (Golden Star): Causes a strong knockback on enemies that collide with the player.
    • Rockets (Golden Flame): Fires a number of homing rockets (equal to number of enemies alive) using the F key. Rockets auto-target and knock enemies off the map.

What I Learned

This prototype helped me expand on earlier concepts and implement slightly more advanced mechanics:

  • Object Tracking and Targeting:
    • Learned how to make an object "track" another object's position by calculating vector direction each frame.
    • Discovered how to use Transform.LookAt() to rotate projectiles (like rockets) toward their moving targets.
  • Powerup Systems:
    • Built temporary, conditional powerups with timers.
    • Developed interaction systems where one pickup can dramatically alter the gameplay loop (e.g., clearing a wave with a single button).
  • Reinforced Skills:
    • Applied previously learned concepts in new ways (e.g., spawning, input handling, physics-based push forces).
    • Learned to combine mechanics in a modular way to form emergent gameplay loops.

Reflection

Dodge-and-Weave was a turning point in understanding how relatively simple behaviors (like tracking and knockback) can be mixed to create strategic tension and replayability. It made me realize how much variety and depth can be achieved with just a few core systems and inspired new ways to think about game design on a small scale.
